1

Credit and interest rates

Banks lend money to households and companies. The borrower pays back the loan plus interest: the interest rate is the price of money. When rates are low, credit is cheap, people borrow more and the economy speeds up. When rates are high, the opposite happens.

You could say:
“When interest rates are low, credit is cheap, so households and firms borrow more. This supports growth, but it can also encourage too much debt.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

2

Bubbles and crises

A bubble appears when the price of an asset, such as houses or shares, rises far above its real value, because everybody expects it to rise again. One day confidence disappears, prices collapse and borrowers cannot repay. The subprime crisis of 2008 followed this cycle: cheap credit, too much risk, then collapse.

You could say:
“This is a typical bubble: prices rose because people expected them to keep rising. When confidence disappeared, the market collapsed.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

3

Systemic risk

Banks lend to each other, so they are linked like dominoes. Systemic risk is the danger that the failure of one bank brings down the others, and then the whole economy. A bank run happens when many savers withdraw their money at the same time because they are afraid.

You could say:
“The danger is systemic risk: if one big bank fails, it can bring down the whole financial system, like dominoes.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

4

Central banks and regulators

A central bank, such as the European Central Bank or the Federal Reserve in the United States, manages the currency: it sets the main interest rate to keep prices stable, and it helps banks in a crisis. Regulators, also called financial watchdogs, write the rules for banks and markets and check that they are respected.

You could say:
“Central banks and regulators act as referees. Their role is to keep prices stable and to prevent a new crisis.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

5

Risk, return and investor protection

On the stock market, investors buy shares: small parts of a company. The basic rule is that a higher possible return always means a higher risk of losing money. Small investors do not always understand this, so the law obliges sellers to inform them clearly.

You could say:
“A high return always comes with a high risk. That is why small investors need clear information and some protection.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

6

Four kinds of money

Not all money is the same. Cash is public money, issued by the central bank. Bank deposits are private money, created by commercial banks when they lend. Cryptocurrencies such as bitcoin are issued by no public authority, and their value changes very fast. A central bank digital currency (CBDC), such as the digital euro project, would be public money in electronic form.

You could say:
“We should not confuse cryptocurrencies with a central bank digital currency. The first are private and volatile; the second would be public money, guaranteed like a banknote.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

7

Strong or weak currency

The exchange rate is the price of one currency in another. With a strong currency, imports are cheaper, which helps consumers and importers, but exports become more expensive for foreign buyers. A weak currency has the opposite effect: it helps exporters but makes imports more expensive.

You could say:
“A strong currency is good news for importers and consumers, but bad news for exporters. A weak currency has the opposite effect.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

8

Money as power

Money is also a political tool. Monetary sovereignty means that a country or a region controls its own currency and its own payment systems. Today, Europeans depend a lot on a few American card networks; a public European system is presented as a way to reduce this dependence.

You could say:
“Money is not only an economic tool; it is also a question of power. A public digital currency could reduce Europe's dependence on foreign payment networks.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

9

Sustainable finance

Investors now judge companies on ESG criteria (environmental, social and governance), not only on profit. A green bond is a loan from investors that must finance an environmental project. In this way, money moves towards more responsible companies.

You could say:
“With sustainable finance, investors look at ESG criteria as well as profit. Finance becomes a tool for the ecological transition.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.
