1

Supply chain

A supply chain is every step needed to make a product and deliver it: raw materials, parts, factories, transport, warehouses, shops. Today these steps are often in different countries, so a problem in one place can stop production in another.

You could say:
“The text shows how fragile global supply chains are. A problem in one country can quickly affect factories all over the world.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

2

Globalisation and offshoring

For about thirty years, companies moved production to countries where wages were lower. This is called offshoring. Bringing production back home is reshoring; moving it to a nearby country is nearshoring.

You could say:
“For a long time, firms chose offshoring to reduce their labour costs. Today, some of them are thinking about reshoring or nearshoring.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

3

Costs, price and margin

A company's margin is the difference between its selling price and its costs. When a cost goes up, the company has two choices: accept a smaller margin, or raise its price. The first reduces profit and investment; the second can reduce sales.

You could say:
“When costs rise, a firm can either absorb them, which reduces its margin, or pass them on to customers, which raises prices.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

4

Tariffs and protectionism

A tariff is a tax on imported goods. Governments use tariffs to protect local producers from foreign competition: this policy is called protectionism. The tax is paid by the importer, and in the end often by the consumer, through higher prices. Other countries usually answer with their own tariffs.

You could say:
“A tariff is a tax on imports. It is supposed to protect local producers, but in the end consumers often pay for it.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

5

Uncertainty and investment

Companies invest when they can predict their future costs and sales. When rules change often, they wait, and investment falls. This is why uncertainty can do as much damage as the tariff itself.

You could say:
“The real problem is not only the tariff, but the uncertainty. If firms cannot predict their costs, they delay their investments.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

6

Just-in-time and stock

Stock costs money: storage, tied-up capital, products that become out of date. Just-in-time production keeps stock very low by receiving parts exactly when they are needed. It is cheap, but any delay can stop the factory. Just-in-case means keeping extra stock for safety.

You could say:
“Just-in-time production saves money, but it only works in a stable world. That is why many firms are moving to a just-in-case approach, with safety stocks.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

7

Dependence, diversification and resilience

Depending on one supplier or one country is risky. Diversification means using several suppliers in several regions. Resilience is the ability to keep working after a shock. It has a price: safety is never free, so firms must choose between lower costs and more security.

You could say:
“To become more resilient, firms diversify their suppliers. However, this safety has a cost, so they must find a balance between efficiency and resilience.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

8

Productivity and automation

Productivity is how much a worker or a factory produces in a given time. Robots and artificial intelligence increase it. With automation, wages matter less in total costs, so producing in a high-wage country becomes possible again.

You could say:
“Thanks to automation, productivity increases and labour costs matter less. This makes it possible to produce again in high-wage countries.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.

9

Sustainability

Producing and transporting goods creates pollution, especially over long distances. Customers and governments now expect companies to reduce their emissions. Producing closer to the customer can lower both emissions and risk.

You could say:
“Producing closer to the customer has two advantages: it reduces transport emissions and it makes the supply chain safer.”

Say it in your own words first, then check.
